Standard vs Refined

The two open-water routers on the Route tab, what each does with the wind and the polar, and what they measured on the same requests on a Raspberry Pi 5 (8 October 2026, one forecast run, fixed departures).

Standard isochrone search

From each surviving point, 61 headings one stage step ahead; the survivors are the best in each of 60 cross-track bins. Repeated for 20 stages. A dead-upwind final leg is tried as a two-tack beat at a few fixed angles.
  1. Corridor first. An A* on the global water grid finds a land-free path; it steers the search and sets the land raster.
  2. Fan of headings. Each surviving point projects 61 headings (±30° about the corridor's bearing, 1° apart) one stage ahead; each leg is simulated with the real polar, wind and current, at 200 m steps.
  3. Prune by position. Candidates are sorted into cross-track bins; one survives per bin, the one with the best elapsed + remaining ÷ cruise. This is where time is lost: a slower branch heading into better wind can be dropped.
  4. Beats by trial. The polar has a no-go angle, so a leg straight into the wind has no speed; the search widens its sweep, and the final approach tries two-tack beats at fixed margins (3°, 8°, 15°, 25°, 40° off the no-go angle).
  5. Tidy up. Straight-line thinning and, optionally, a shortcut smoother that only deletes points.

Refined the same search, worked over

The Standard search runs underneath; these three steps change what it sees (the hull) and what happens to its answer (legs laid out, polish).
  1. Convex polar. Per wind speed, the polar curve is replaced by its convex hull. Into the wind the hull has speed, the beat's VMG, so a leg straight upwind is a valid, fast leg and the search needs no beat tricks. The same across a dead run: the hull says "gybe".
  2. Search on the hull. The Standard search runs unchanged on that hull polar, corridor and bins included.
  3. Legs laid forward in time. Every sailed leg is walked in steps of at most 5 nm. At each step the wind there and then decides: if the course to the leg's end is a mix of two polar headings, a tack is placed (alternating sides, the last landing on the end); if the polar sails it directly, one straight step. So a wind that veers along a long leg is met where it veers. A tack that crosses land or that the real polar can't sail is tried the other way, then shorter; if nothing works the route fails and says so. No leg ever gets an invented time.
  4. Cross-track polish. Each inner waypoint is tried a little to either side; a move stays when the route, re-timed from there with the real polar, arrives earlier and both legs stay clear of land. Up to five passes.

Reading it: on the beat, the Moderate method alone finds more than the Refined router does, and the two don't add up. On the reach only the Refined router's polish moves the number, by a few minutes. On the passage they stack: Refined with Moderate equals Standard with Maximum in half the time. The Normal method's pruning is the larger loss on long passages; the Method choice is the direct remedy.
